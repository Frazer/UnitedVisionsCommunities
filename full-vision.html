From the Transcript

What the Main Page Leaves Out

01

Place, Nature & Architecture

In touch with nature, archways with hanging barriers, shared gyms and a movie theater — a peaceful, blissful physical home.

A beautiful place in touch with nature — not only gardens, but land and living systems felt in daily life.

Archways with barriers hanging down as you walk from building to building — a soft, intimate way of moving through the community (the main page says "hanging gardens"; the transcript describes barriers hanging down).

Shared amenities: gyms, sports grounds, and a movie theater — richness of life without everyone owning everything separately.

Visitors should experience a peaceful, blissful place made beautiful through architecture, fresh healthy food, flowers, and art — not only ideas, but a felt physical experience.

02

Creative Culture — Extended

Video games, live-streamed Masaya Jams, fashion that teaches truth — and warming up into your most expressive self.

Actively aiming to succeed at creating: fashion lines, video games, TV shows and movies, writing books, and podcasts — culture meant to be exported.

Masaya Jams with live-streaming cameras on — everyone encouraged to role-play the kindest, most awesome, wildest version of themselves, like theater games oriented toward enlightenment.

"The kinds of things that would come out of you as you warm up will be awesome."

Not only words: someone might suddenly propose a VR world and a group builds it in an afternoon.

A fashion line where imagery helps people feel love for all beings — or teaches something about rationality, questioning, and truth.

Times when creativity is allowed to inspire and uplift the whole community — and that becomes normal culture.

03

Schools, Food & Daily Culture

Kids play sport and visualize being awesome; delicious food; community events; uplifting lyrics and art everywhere.

Schools where kids learn mindfulness and kindness, rehearse confidence and handling scenarios — and go play sport while everybody visualizes being awesome.

Contrast with schools where only some kids rehearse success while others mentally rehearse being terrible — a culture actively helping everyone's mind be awesome is a very different culture.

Workplaces and teams that practice the same orientation; fashion and music where everybody cares about positive impact.

  • Lyrics and words that leave everyone feeling lifted up
  • Art around the community that is intentionally inspiring
  • Food that is delicious and healthy
  • Loving, supportive friendships and relationships
  • Community events — everyone encouraged to think about and create them

An enriching, uplifting society — and encouragement for every intentional community to share what they do so more people can find communities that uplift them.

04

Ethics — As Spoken

Don't buy from slavery or harm; kindness to animals and the environment — stated in the raw terms of the talk.

A really ethical community: when buying things, don't buy stuff from slaves or places where anyone was hurt — and where any animals were harmed — and be kind to the environment.

People excited not only to help each other but to have a positive impact on the world — so creative culture built together is something we can export with integrity.

05

Finding & Growing Community

VR Chat, events and businesses together, exploring ic.org, practicing dreams — and inviting Frazer into aligned spaces.

"Where does that exist?" — maybe more events in VR Chat; also living with events and businesses together.

Please invite me to more events and groups that line up with this vision. Likewise, if you know intentional communities or companies that align, let Frazer know — and be a resource: help people follow their dreams and find their team.

In VR you can see someone's groups; in the wider world it's often luck whether you discover something awesome. Want more ways in our culture to share the stuff that's awesome — many friends invited across communities to create overlap.

Putting positivity out there wherever you can is a wonderful quality.

Your intentional community might be a meditation group or friends who party together positively — whatever you're imagining, Frazer wanted to hear it.

Explore via intentionalcommunities.org (ic.org) — visit local meditation communities, dance clubs, or communities worldwide.

Practice your dreams — visiting real places shows whether your hopes feel good in practice; visions change when tested.

06

Frazer's Journey & Evolving Vision

Permaculture in Australia, preferring the city, society as commune — and using Twitter/art to make the vision visible.

Ran a permaculture community in Australia — lots of garden time — and realized: doesn't want to be a farmer; prefers living close to a city. Gardening can be done by people who love it; Frazer wants to interact with lots of people.

Younger dreams of complete self-sustaining communes matter less now. Cares more about society being environmentally friendly and feeling more connected.

Might live on a farm if the community were meditative and enlightening enough, retreats were great, and impact was wonderful — but broadly wants intentional communities everywhere, people exploring, creating, moving, and connecting with ideal people.

Doesn't tweet much; handle is @Frazer Kirkman (name). This session was meant as a kickoff to write it down, tweet about it, and create art so people can see what he sees.

Mentioned communities in conversation: e.g. Penguins Meadow Farm (Mendocino), California as a good region, and interest in the Helping Hands community (including sign language).

07

Attention & Beliefs

Time is precious; belief systems can be refined — including learning to speak openly and clearly.

"Our time is precious."

Limited minutes, limited space for thoughts and conversations. The universe heads in a direction shaped by what you put your attention on — that decides who you hang out with, how you feel, how you see things; collectively, that shapes the world.

Embrace how precious attention is: what you put time into, what feelings you feed, what ideas you contemplate and grow — for yourself and others. Find skillful navigation; refine belief systems that help, transform those that hurt.

Imagine what's best, then create the mindset and perspective that brings more of it — how you want to feel, interact, and how easy or empowered you want your mind to be.

Grew up where talking openly about even slightly confronting things was nearly impossible — communication and understanding suffered. Confronted that belief; imagined a world where saying what you mean is easy — and found everything improved. Invites us to reflect on beliefs we think are true and refine them.

08

Music, Meditation & Expression

Immersive piano vs intentional practice; music's power; recording, art, dance, and guiding meditations.

Immersive activity (e.g. piano) isn't automatically meditation — intentionality matters: mindful playing, enriching the music, or compassion flowing through the activity. Music isn't a waste of time; it's a skill that can serve many ends.

Music can be powerfully emotive — uplifting or harmful depending on lyrics and impact; some art lifts more people, some harms more. Hope for creators intentionally aiming for the best possible impact on the most people. Even difficult material can become a springboard for compassion when met with mindfulness; still, tend toward what lifts more people.

Expression tips from the session:

  • Writing, sticky notes, big posters — or recording yourself talking (or Q&A) and transcribing — talking flows for Frazer
  • Art, dance, walking in nature as contexts that free expression
  • Guiding meditations — inner state unlocks words and feeling beyond the ordinary
  • Meditating then writing; movement as expression

Meditation as often as possible — walking, talking, loving, learning — choosing mindset amid obstacles.

09

The Om Story & United Visions

Moonlight on a Tibetan mountain — U and V that were really Om — and what that symbol means.

While staying at a Tibetan Buddhist temple (where United Visions' charity logo — U and V — first lived in Frazer's life), he climbed their sacred mountain by moonlight toward a stupa.

On a plateau, an engraved stone showed symbols of major religious movements — and at the top, U and V. It felt staggering, as if the world had been waiting.

By daylight it was the Tibetan Om — other marks hidden in moon shadow. Om still carries special meaning: a world where everyone is mindful, choosing what to rehearse, master of body, feelings, and consciousness.

Frazer doesn't insist on mysticism but likes how meaning can intensify through what people believe and practice together.

10

The Thought Experiment

If everything were already how you wanted — struggles gone, community supporting you — what would you do now?

Facilitated in the session (also on the main page CTA):

Imagine you have everything you wanted. Everything works the way you like. Struggles and wasted time are gone. Your community helps you be awesome. What would your awesome life be like?

Make it stronger: What would I choose then? This is me now. I'm becoming my best self right now. As you start doing more of that, you bring more of that community toward you and make things better and better.

Someone in the room impacted Frazer deeply with this framing — the question is offered here in full so it can be sat with slowly, not only as a headline.

11

Closing the Session

Gratitude, true nature, social time — and gratitude for everyone who shared.

Instead of a full closing meditation, a contemplation on gratitude — things from today, reminders felt more powerfully, mindfulness, mental rehearsal, wisdom, practice.

Consider using challenges to find something even stronger inside yourself — grateful for anything that comes your way.

Springboard from a participant: "My true nature is nothing but nature."

Thank you for time shared; option to socialize or keep meditating after leaving the temple space.
